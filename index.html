The pitch

Tag-team through the workday

Bugs, scope creep, meeting invites and Slack pings come at you from every side. You lead a squad of three. Your hero fights on its own, so your thumbs do the thinking: who should be out there, and when to hit POWER.

It is built for the gap between meetings. One run is about three minutes, there is nothing to learn before you start, and every power is bigger than you expect.

How it plays

Three ideas, no tutorial wall

  1. Match the colour

    Every enemy health bar is split into colours: CODE, DESIGN, PROCESS, TEST. A hero hits its own colour 2.4x harder and the wrong one barely scratches it. A squad of three can never cover all four, so swapping heroes is the game.

    CODEDESIGNPROCESSTEST

  2. Charge it, then smash POWER

    One shared meter fills a pip every four seconds. Fire at one, two or three pips: each tier is bigger than the last. Three pips is a screen wrecker, and every hero has a different one.

  3. Call in a DRI

    When the biggest incident hits, a Directly Responsible Individual drops in for 18 seconds, hits every colour equally and clears most of the wave. Then they leave. You still have to survive the rest.

Design decisions

What shaped it

  • Strategy over reflexesAuto-attack and one thumb stick keep the hands simple. The skill is picking who is out there and when to fire POWER, so a thoughtful player beats a twitchy one.
  • Enemies are day-to-day problemsNobody needs a tutorial for a Meeting Invite. Each enemy wears a tiny badge and name, so the joke and the colour are readable at a glance.
  • Instant gratificationNo menus before the first fight. Powers are huge on purpose, and the score screen counts up like an arcade cabinet.
  • Calm screen, loud momentsThe world is soft and spaced out so that surges, bosses and powers are what pop. Names stay big while the camera zooms out.
  • Made for phones firstLandscape, big thumb targets, no hover, offline after the first load, and an Add to Home Screen install with no store.
How it was built

Small pieces, tested hard

  • No engine, no dependenciesPlain JavaScript modules and a canvas. A player can load it in a couple of seconds and it runs on an old phone.
  • Art is recombined, not drawn from scratchFree CC0 vector character parts, hue shifted per colour tag and composited by a script into twelve heroes and a full enemy roster.
  • Balanced by robotsHeadless bots play the whole game at different skill levels. A player who stands still takes about six times the damage of a good one, so skill matters but nobody is locked out.
  • Rules live in plain functionsDamage, scoring, unlocks and level progress are small pure functions with 45 automated tests, so changes do not quietly break the maths.
  • Reviewed and hardenedA code review caught real problems before release: offline caching, blocked storage, a keyboard edge case in the name editor and a tie in the scoreboard.
